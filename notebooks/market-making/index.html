Market Making — Numerical Illustrations

This notebook supports chapters Market Making Fundamentals and Optimal Market Making. It covers:

  1. Grossman-Miller model: inventory-risk spread vs competition, volatility, trade size and risk aversion.

  2. Glosten-Milgrom model: information-asymmetry spread vs informed fraction.

  3. AS/GLF parameter sensitivities: static spread and skew coefficient vs all model parameters in one consolidated figure.

  4. AS Backtesting (following Avellaneda & Stoikov 2008): single simulated day comparing GLF stationary strategy vs single-trade EOD-liquidation strategy vs unmanaged symmetric quotes.

  5. Monte Carlo P&L distributions: daily P&L distributions across 500 simulated paths for each strategy.

3. Avellaneda–Stoikov / GLF: Parameter Sensitivities¶

The Guéant-Lehalle-Fernández-Tapia stationary approximation gives:

  • Static half-spread: η=1γln⁡ ⁣(1+γk)\eta = \frac{1}{\gamma}\ln\!\left(1+\frac{\gamma}{k}\right)η=γ1​ln(1+kγ​)

  • Skew coefficient: θ=γσ22Aln⁡(1+γ/k)\theta = \frac{\gamma\sigma^2}{\sqrt{2A\ln(1+\gamma/k)}}θ=2Aln(1+γ/k)​γσ2​

  • Optimal quotes: pa∗=S+η−θqp^{a*} = S + \eta - \theta qpa∗=S+η−θq, pb∗=S−η−θqp^{b*} = S - \eta - \theta qpb∗=S−η−θq

4. AS Backtesting: Simulating a Trading Day¶

We simulate the Avellaneda-Stoikov (2008) model with two active strategies and one passive benchmark:

GLF strategy (stationary approximation): quotes at reservation price rt=St−θqtr_t = S_t - \theta q_trt​=St​−θqt​ with constant half-spread η\etaη.

Single-trade (EOD) strategy: at each step ttt with remaining horizon τ=T−t\tau = T-tτ=T−t, the dealer prices each order opportunity using the single-trade formula:

δa(t,q)=max⁡ ⁣(1k+γσ2τ2(1−2q), ϵ),δb(t,q)=max⁡ ⁣(1k+γσ2τ2(1+2q), ϵ)\delta^{a}(t,q) = \max\!\left(\frac{1}{k} + \frac{\gamma\sigma^2\tau}{2}(1 - 2q),\, \epsilon\right), \quad \delta^{b}(t,q) = \max\!\left(\frac{1}{k} + \frac{\gamma\sigma^2\tau}{2}(1 + 2q),\, \epsilon\right)δa(t,q)=max(k1​+2γσ2τ​(1−2q),ϵ),δb(t,q)=max(k1​+2γσ2τ​(1+2q),ϵ)
(3)

This is derived in chapter Optimal Market Making (single-trade model, §3) and is also the leading-order finite-horizon approximation from the AS paper. The spread is wider early in the day (large τ\tauτ) and converges to 2/k2/k2/k by end of day as inventory risk becomes irrelevant.

Symmetric benchmark: fixed spread 2η2\eta2η around mid with no inventory management. Inventory accumulates as a random walk.

Terminal wealth includes an EOD liquidation penalty Aliq qT2A_{\text{liq}}\, q_T^2Aliq​qT2​ for any remaining inventory.

def simulate_as(T, n_steps, S0, sigma, A, k, gamma, strategy='glf',
                A_liq=None, seed=0):
    """
    Simulate one trading day under an AS market-making strategy.

    Parameters
    ----------
    strategy : 'glf' | 'single_trade' | 'symmetric'
    A_liq    : float or None — quadratic EOD inventory penalty; default 1/(2k)

    Returns
    -------
    S_path, q_path, bid_path, ask_path, pnl (scalar), mtm_path
    """
    dt  = T / n_steps
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)

    log_gk = np.log(1 + gamma / k)
    eta    = log_gk / gamma
    theta  = gamma * sigma**2 / np.sqrt(2 * A * log_gk)
    if A_liq is None:
        A_liq = 1.0 / (2 * k)

    S, q, X = float(S0), 0, 0.0

    S_path   = np.zeros(n_steps + 1)
    q_path   = np.zeros(n_steps + 1, dtype=int)
    bid_path = np.zeros(n_steps)
    ask_path = np.zeros(n_steps)
    mtm      = np.zeros(n_steps + 1)
    S_path[0] = S;  mtm[0] = 0.0

    for step in range(n_steps):
        tau = T - step * dt

        if strategy == 'glf':
            delta_a = max(eta - theta * q, 1e-4)
            delta_b = max(eta + theta * q, 1e-4)

        elif strategy == 'single_trade':
            ir = gamma * sigma**2 * tau          # inventory-risk term
            delta_a = max(1/k + ir * (0.5 - q), 1e-4)
            delta_b = max(1/k + ir * (0.5 + q), 1e-4)

        else:  # symmetric
            delta_a = delta_b = eta

        bid = S - delta_b
        ask = S + delta_a
        bid_path[step] = bid
        ask_path[step] = ask

        # Bernoulli approximation to Poisson arrivals
        p_b = min(A * np.exp(-k * delta_b) * dt, 0.9)  # prob of buy (client buys at ask)
        p_a = min(A * np.exp(-k * delta_a) * dt, 0.9)  # prob of sell (client sells at bid)

        u = rng.random(2)
        if u[0] < p_b:    # client buys → MM sells at ask
            X += ask;  q -= 1
        if u[1] < p_a:    # client sells → MM buys at bid
            X -= bid;  q += 1

        S += sigma * np.sqrt(dt) * rng.standard_normal()
        S_path[step + 1]  = S
        q_path[step + 1]  = q
        mtm[step + 1]     = X + q * S

    pnl = X + q * S - A_liq * q**2
    return S_path, q_path, bid_path, ask_path, pnl, mtm

Reading the single-day simulation:

GLF (left column): the bid-ask band translates rigidly up and down following the reservation price rt=St−θqtr_t = S_t - \theta q_trt​=St​−θqt​, but maintains a constant width 2η2\eta2η throughout the day. When inventory runs positive the whole band shifts down, attracting buyers; when negative it shifts up. The spread income per trade is constant.

Single-trade / EOD (right column): the band widens early in the day (large τ\tauτ, large inventory-risk component in the spread) and narrows as close approaches (τ→0\tau \to 0τ→0, spread converges to 2/k2/k2/k). The skew is also time-varying: early in the day the skew sensitivity γσ2τ\gamma\sigma^2\tauγσ2τ is large, so the MM leans heavily against inventory; near close the skew vanishes regardless of inventory level (forcing EOD liquidation to handle the residual). This time-varying spread is wider on average than the GLF spread, resulting in fewer fills but higher margin per fill.

Reading the spread dynamics figure:

Left panel — total spread vs remaining time τ\tauτ:

  • GLF quotes a constant spread 2η2\eta2η regardless of how much time remains in the day — it operates as a stationary market maker with no intraday urgency.

  • The single-trade strategy quotes a spread that narrows monotonically as the day ends (τ→0\tau \to 0τ→0), converging to 2/k2/k2/k (the pure static component with no inventory risk). Early in the day, with large τ\tauτ, the spread includes the full inventory-risk premium and is substantially wider than the GLF spread.

  • For a long inventory (q=+5q=+5q=+5), the ask half-spread narrows more aggressively (the market maker wants to sell) while the bid half-spread widens, explaining the change in spread with qqq.

Right panel — skew (δa−δb\delta^a - \delta^bδa−δb) vs inventory at different horizons:

  • Both strategies produce a linear skew in inventory — the signature feature of inventory management.

  • The single-trade skew slope is −γσ2τ-\gamma\sigma^2\tau−γσ2τ and shrinks to zero as τ→0\tau \to 0τ→0 (end of day: no more inventory management, all positions liquidated at market).

  • The GLF skew slope is −2θ-2\theta−2θ (constant) and is calibrated to the stationary arrival rate AAA, not to the remaining time. Near the start of the day, the single-trade strategy is more aggressive; near the close, GLF maintains its skew while single-trade skew vanishes.

P&L comparison (previous figure): both active strategies substantially outperform the unmanaged symmetric benchmark on a risk-adjusted basis. The GLF strategy tends to produce a slightly higher mean P&L due to its tighter average spread (more fills) at the cost of a fat left tail when the day ends with large inventory. The single-trade strategy has a lower mean (wider spread, fewer fills) but tighter tails (EOD liquidation is priced in explicitly from the start).
